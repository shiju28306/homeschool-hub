Subitising to 5

Ages about 4–5

What this teaches: recognising how many are in a small group without counting each dot. This is called subitising.

What comes next: spot familiar patterns on a die, then explore two groups that make five with our Number Bonds to 5 pack.

What you need: a pencil and a spare sheet to cover the pictures. Print in black and white.

Try it together: a short parent guide

  1. Start with a group of 1, 2 or 3 dots. Cover the other pictures so your child can focus on one group.
  2. Show the group briefly, then cover it and ask, “How many did you see?” Uncover it together to check.
  3. Ask, “How did you see it?” Let your child count if needed. Model a small group: “I saw two together and one more. That makes three.”
  4. Move to groups of 4 and 5 as confidence grows. Your child can say the answer while you write it if number writing is still difficult.

Try a few groups for about five minutes, stopping sooner if your child is tired. This is a noticing game, not a speed test. The printable asks children not to count one by one because quick recognition is the goal; counting to check is still welcome while learning.

Checking answers: page 8 lists answers for pages 3–7 in top, middle and bottom order. Keep that page covered during the activity.
